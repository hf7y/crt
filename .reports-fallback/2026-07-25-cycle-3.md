## 1. Your note on the mid-utterance duck — `f13c7a4`

You flagged the "Observation, deliberately not changed" bullet: the VAD checks
`MUTED` only when *starting* an utterance, so a duck arriving mid-utterance
doesn't stop buffering. Fixed.

The previous cycle's reason for leaving it — "changing when an utterance gets
truncated is a by-ear tuning call" — was reasoning about the wrong fix. There
are three possible behaviours, not two:

| | what whisper gets |
|---|---|
| buffer the ducked audio (what it did) | our own earcon/reply, transcribed as the speaker's words |
| close the utterance at the duck | truncation — and with `CRT_EARCON_ON_THRESHOLD=1` the `heard` earcon fires *at onset*, so this cuts every utterance to nothing |
| **freeze and excise (landed)** | speech either side of the duck, spliced |

Only the middle one is the by-ear call. Freezing isn't: ducked chunks are
dropped and the silence timer stops advancing while they are, so playback
neither enters the buffer nor ends the utterance. A duck outlasting
`CRT_MUTE_UTT_MAX_SECS` (new, 2s, 0 disables) closes the utterance normally,
so a long spoken reply can't hold one open.

Excising can drop real speech if you talk straight through the duck. That's
deliberate, and it leans on the measurement the duck exists for: this adapter
records its own playback at ~0.1x, so those frames are mostly playback anyway,
and a short splice is kinder to whisper than a beep sitting inside the
sentence. **The 2.0s bound is a guess** — it's the third by-ear number on the
pile with `CRT_WAKE_ARM_SECS` and `CRT_WAKE_ARM_MAX_SECS`.

Witness: `tests/test_duck_midutterance_excision.sh` drives the real CLI with a
fake capture that ducks itself mid-sentence and measures the WAV handed to
whisper. 400cs at the parent commit, 300cs here, and the truncating
alternative would be 110cs — all three outcomes are distinguishable, so the
test pins which one is in force, not just "something changed". Plus 8 cases in
`test_stt_solo_helpers.py` on the extracted pure decision function
(`utt_chunk()`).

`STT-MECHANISM.md` gained a paragraph on this, since "the console talked over
you and the word straddling that moment lost its middle" is now a real
explanation for a garbled transcription that a future instance should know
about before blaming whisper.

## 2. The duck was reachable from fewer places than anyone thought — `9063661`

Auditing every playback path in `bin/` for the above turned up the more
serious defect. The duck fired on `device == "handset"` — a string compare, in
a project whose entire audio history is devices identified by the wrong name
(`plughw:0,0`, `CRT_CTL_FILE` never exported, "KT USB Audio" moving cards).

Three callers never name a device, fall through to ALSA `default`, and so
**skipped the duck entirely**:

| caller | live? |
|---|---|
| `crt-secretary.py`'s `play_earcon("thinking")` | **yes** — `crt-console.sh:193` boots the stt window with `CRT_STT_SINK=secretary` |
| `crt-stt-speakback.sh`'s TTS | debug tool, not booted |
| `crt-idle-teaser.sh`'s `chime()` | not wired into `crt-console.sh` |

`--device plughw:1,0` — naming the handset outright — skipped it too. And
`crt-console.sh` pins `CRT_AUDIO_DEV=plughw:1,0` for capture while the handset
output default *is* `plughw:1,0`, so the same-card premise the whole duck rests
on is confirmed in the boot path itself.

Both producers now resolve the device first and decide from the hardware.
**Unknown (`default`/empty) now ducks**, which reverses the previous explicit
intent ("tv/default playback must NOT touch the CTL file"). Nothing anywhere
establishes what `default` is on potato, and the two errors aren't symmetric:
an unneeded duck costs one earcon of suppressed VAD and clears itself twice
over (end of playback, then `CRT_CTL_MUTE_MAX_SECS`); a missing one puts the
console's own voice into whisper. Explicitly naming a non-capture device still
doesn't duck. `crt-stt-speakback.sh` now says `--device handset`, which its own
header always claimed it did.

The two commits compose the way you'd want: the "thinking" earcon fires just
after an utterance ends, so it's the *start* gate that catches it — but a
chime landing mid-utterance is now both ducked and excised rather than
transcribed.

## New issues found

- **The duck's keying, not its mechanism, was the open half of bar item 2.**
  Generalises past this fix: any future "is this the handset?" check that
  compares a caller-supplied label rather than a resolved device will have the
  same hole. The resolution helpers (`resolve_alsa_device`/`ducks_capture` in
  `crt-tts.py`) are the place to add to.
- **A test that skips itself on a missing tool is an exit-0 no-op**, and this
  project's suite had three. Worth a rule: fake the tool, or fail — don't
  skip. I did not sweep the rest of the repo for this pattern beyond the
  `sox` cases; `test_sideband.sh`'s remaining skip is legitimate.
- **`crt-stt-solo.py` reads its capture pipe strictly in order**, so `MUTED`
  (live wall-clock) can be up to ~2s out of phase with the audio it's applied
  to, bounded by the 64KB pipe. Pre-existing — the start gate has always had
  it — and the lag only opens while `transcribe()` blocks, then closes as the
  loop catches up at full speed. Not changed; noted because §1's excision now
  depends on that alignment where nothing did before.
- **Possible mild regression, flagged not fixed:** a chime played on
  `default` now ducks, so if someone answers an idle-bait chime *within* its
  ~0.3s the start of their reply is gated where before it was captured (mixed
  with the chime). Judged the better trade, but it's a real behaviour change
  on an unwired path.
