## 2. A question nobody was asked, graded anyway — `2776f99`

`crt-book-answer-listen.py` decides a question is pending from the scan
timestamp alone: any utterance within `CRT_BOOK_ANSWER_WINDOW_SECS` (20s) of
the most recent scan gets graded against that book's question, written to
`book-game-training.jsonl`, and announced on the tube. Nothing anywhere
recorded that the round had already been answered, so the window kept
grading for its full twenty seconds.

That is not a hypothetical second answer. `crt-stt-solo.py:1333` writes
**every** recognized utterance to `stt.log`, *before* the wake gate — this
window is the one consumer in the project that sees unaddressed room speech,
and CLAUDE.md's whole premise is a room with ambient chatter in it. So the
second thing graded was whatever anyone said next:

```
scan → "fiction" → tube: "got it!" → "nice, next one"
     → tube: "nope, it was fiction"
     → {"expected": "fiction", "heard": "nice next one"}
```

The 2026-07-21 fix caught that utterance only when it is a recognized voice
command (`find_playbook`). Ordinary speech is not a command, and ordinary
speech is most of what a room contains.

`book-game-training.jsonl` is the artifact this whole subsystem exists to
produce — FOCUS.md's 2026-07-21 end-goal statement. A row whose `heard` was
never an answer attempt is worse than a missing row: it is mislabelled
training data. And it does not just sit there. `crt-stt-training-merge.py`
recomputes candidate fixups from exactly these mismatches every 600s, so a
garbage pair recurring twice becomes an `auto` entry in `stt-fixups.json`.
Harmless today only because the gate acts on entries whose intent is the wake
word — which is cycle 10's still-open question 5, now with a live source of
junk feeding it.

`mark_answered()` (`crt-book-game.py:632`) is the counterpart to
`touch_scan()`: a scan opens a round, grading closes it. Two choices inside
it worth your eye:

- Readers compare `last_answered` against the book's **own scan timestamp**,
  not against the clock — so a re-scan re-opens the round with nothing to
  clear, and cycle 12's fix stays intact (three tests pin exactly that).
- The answered check runs **after** `LIMIT 1`, not as a `WHERE` clause.
  Filtering in SQL would let a closed round fall through to the
  previously-scanned book if it were still inside its own window, grading
  against a question that is not the one on the tube — precisely the
  corrupted row cycle 12 fixed. The most recent scan is the question on
  screen; if that one is closed, nothing is pending.

Existing rows have `last_answered` NULL and behave exactly as they do today.
No backfill. The `--answer` CLI closes the round too, so a typed answer stops
the next spoken utterance being graded against the question it just answered.

**15 tests** (`tests/test_book_answer_round_closes.py`). **11 fail against
the parent**, the headline one by finding two training rows where a person
gave one answer, reading `heard='nice next one'`. The other four are the
don't-break-what-works half — the first utterance still grades, a command
still doesn't consume the round, a re-scan still re-opens it.

## 3. Stress-tested, not just green

The real hazard in adding a column to `books` is positional row access
somewhere else. Checked all of it: `get_book()` builds a dict from
`cursor.description` so it is column-order-independent, and every other
reader (`crt-book-catalog.py`, `crt-book-game-stats.py`,
`crt-wake-pool.py`, `crt-book-console.py`) names its columns explicitly.
Nothing reads `books` positionally.

On the two-writer question this project keeps running into: `books.db` is WAL
with a 10s busy timeout, and both writes are short `UPDATE`s. The ordering
inside `grade_pending_answer` is deliberate — **close the round before
logging the row**. If that `UPDATE` fails, nothing has been written yet, and
the raise reaches `main()`'s `LoopGuard` and gets reported on window 1 rather
than leaving a logged row behind an open round that will grade again.
